01 An operator's view

Work directly with Ted Watkin.

Distilled Advisory is led by Edward (Ted) Watkin, with spirits sales and brand advocacy experience at Bacardi and Brown-Forman. His previous roles inform a practical approach to pricing, buyer preparation and account development.

Ask the questions a buyer will ask, before the buyer asks them.
Operating principle

Start with the buyer, the producer and the unit cost. Then agree the priorities, responsibilities and scope before the work begins.

Practical support can include buyer conversations, tastings, staff training and account follow-up where agreed. Each project has a defined market and remit. The retainer below is capped decision support.

02 What we do

The work, in three lanes.

Three disciplines, applied independently or together. Advisory and implementation responsibilities are agreed in the engagement scope.

01 · Production and procurement

Lower unit cost. Shorter lead times.

Supplier evaluation and introductions across distilling, manufacturing and packaging. Compare capability, lead times, unit costs and commercial terms. Production is delivered by the selected suppliers.

02 · Commercial structure

Pricing, margin, terms.

Pricing across distributor, on-premise and retail tiers. Margin modelling, allocation and reorder terms that show what each channel needs to contribute.

03 · Route to market

Where to play. How to enter. Who to enter through.

Distributor selection, channel priorities and account targeting, built from buyer back to brand. Single-state focus where it sharpens the answer. Two states maximum.

03 Engagement structures

Five named engagements. One firm.

Choose a starting point for the decision in front of you. Scope, responsibilities and fees are agreed before work begins; fees are discussed after the first meeting.

Discovery Sprint

Two to three weeks

A scoped diagnostic. Half-day workshop, supplier and buyer interviews, written read-out. A clear view of where the brand stands and the single next step we would take.

Deliverables
  • Diagnostic note
  • Recommended next step
  • Optional engagement scope

Commercial Reset

Six to eight weeks
Default engagement

For brands with stock to clear, distribution that has stalled, or a route to market that is not paying for itself. Pricing, partner terms and reorder plans rebuilt for the next twelve to eighteen months.

Deliverables
  • Tiered pricing
  • Partner shortlist
  • Reorder mechanics
  • Buyer collateral

Route-to-Market Review

Four to six weeks

For brands early in market or moving between markets. The route is rebuilt from buyer back to brand. Single-state focus is the default. Depth in one market beats a thin presence in three.

Deliverables
  • Route map
  • Distributor recommendation
  • Account targeting
  • Terms framework

Buyer-Readiness Preparation

One to three weeks

For brands going into a major buyer conversation. National accounts, group buyers, on-premise group submissions. The pitch is rebuilt from the buyer's evaluation criteria backwards.

Deliverables
  • Pitch pack
  • Buyer-criteria mapping
  • Prep session
  • Optional in-room attendance

Operator Retainer

Three-month minimum, rolling

For founders who want a senior operator on the line for ongoing decision support without a full engagement. Capped at twelve hours per month. Pricing calls, distributor escalations, supplier conversations, pre-meeting prep.

Channels
  • Phone
  • WhatsApp
  • Document review
  • On-demand meetings
04 Who this is for

Commercially open. Capital ready. Value oriented.

01 · Commercially open

Founders who want plain answers.

Founders who want a commercial read of what they have built, not validation of it. Willing to change direction when the evidence asks them to.

02 · Capital ready

Capital placed or about to be placed.

Brands with capital placed or about to be placed. Family offices, investor groups, and well-prepared founder-led businesses. We protect that capital with the decisions that come before it is spent.

03 · Value oriented

Building equity, not headlines.

Operators who measure progress in equity, distribution depth, and category position. Building a business that lasts, not a brand that trends.

05 The market problem

Good products need a commercial plan.

A promising product still needs pricing that works, reliable supply and a route to the right accounts. A listing alone does not make the business sustainable.

Distilled helps founders examine those decisions together, before committing capital or expanding into another market.

06 How we support founders

Experience. Access. Judgment.

01 · Experience

Experience in spirits and hospitality.

Commercial advice informed by spirits sales and brand advocacy, with attention to the account, the buyer and the people serving the product.

02 · Access

A working network, not a Rolodex.

Relevant supplier and commercial introductions, made carefully and when the brand is ready. An introduction is a starting point; preparation and follow-through matter.

03 · Judgment

The unflashy answer.

A clear view of the options, costs and trade-offs, including when the better decision is to wait.

07 The pathway

Foundations. Readiness. Launch & scale.

Engagements move through three phases. Founders can join at any one. Most benefit from beginning earlier than they think.

01

Foundations

Commercial structure, pricing and margin, brand positioning, and the founding distribution plan. The work built before the first conversation with a buyer. Six to twelve weeks.

02

Readiness

Buyer materials, sample programs, trade narrative, and the operating rhythm to support a launch. We pressure-test the brand against what a senior buyer will pull apart in the room.

03

Launch & scale

Distribution execution, account-by-account work, partner management, and the discipline to grow without losing margin. Support is scoped to the stage, priorities and capacity agreed for each engagement.

08 Results, not noise

Three outcomes the work is built to deliver.

01 · Time to market

A clearer path to market.

Prioritise the decisions, buyer preparation and supply requirements that stand between the brand and its next market.

02 · Capital protection

The right spend, in the right order.

Capital deployed on the things that move the business. Distribution chosen for the brand, not the broker. Margin held through the channel rather than gifted to it.

03 · Scalable supply chain

A supply position that holds.

Assess capacity, lead times and costs against the growth plan, so supplier choices account for what comes next.
